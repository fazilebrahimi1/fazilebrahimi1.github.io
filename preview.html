Featured project

Apr – Jun 2026 · MSc Capstone · Client: Rebel Solutions Group · Grade A

Uncovering and predicting revenue loss in public transport

Built a payment-failure prediction system for a European public transport operator.

  • Data pipeline: turned raw transaction logs into 42 features, with strict time-based controls so no future information leaked into training.
  • Lightweight logistic regression for real-time checks at the gate, flagging high-risk accounts without slowing passengers down.
  • Nightly XGBoost model (AUC 0.797) to prioritise back-office collection and denial-list queues.
  • Dynamic threshold matrix balancing passenger friction against financial risk, recovering 27.6% of baseline uncollected revenue.
  • Delivered a technical report, business and academic presentations, and a reproducible notebook.

MSc · Time Series Forecasting

Rossmann store sales forecasting

Forecast weekly sales for a Rossmann drugstore using real retail data (Kaggle, 2013–2015), comparing SARIMA, ARIMA, and XGBoost with lag and promotion features.

  • XGBoost was the most accurate model, with an 8.4% error (MAPE), against 12.0% for ARIMA and 18.2% for SARIMA.
  • Promotions added about €6,800 in weekly revenue, a 28% uplift.

2026 · CEU AI Engineering hackathon

AI travel agent: multi-agent trip planner

A trip planner built with the OpenAI Agents SDK, where a planner agent delegates to two specialist agents. It started as a team project at the CEU AI Engineering hackathon, and I then rebuilt and extended it as my own standalone version.

  • RAG: recommends destinations from a guide stored in ChromaDB.
  • Live tools: weather forecasts (Open-Meteo) and currency conversion (ECB rates).
  • Guardrail and memory: stays on travel topics and remembers budget and dates during the chat.
  • Runs on any model through LiteLLM (Gemini, OpenAI, Claude, Bedrock), with a Chainlit chat interface.
  • Packaged in a dev container, tested automatically with GitHub Actions on every push, and set up for one-click deployment on Render.

MSc · Causal Analysis · Term project

Does higher internet penetration cause lower unemployment?

Tested whether expanding internet access reduces unemployment across countries, using World Bank panel data and regressions with country and year fixed effects. The average effect was not statistically significant, but splitting by income level showed the effect is about four times stronger in high-income countries, suggesting the benefits of internet access depend on a country's institutions.

Determinants of trust in government

Analysed 1,683 survey respondents across Balkan countries with a probit model in Stata, testing 17 socio-demographic, economic, and political variables. Transparency, local authorities, and rule of law were the strongest significant predictors.

CEU Hackathon · Organised by SDG Watch Austria · For the City of Vienna · Winner, Challenge 3

Vienna FoodPrint: app prototype & policy frameworks

In a team of three, we worked on a challenge for the Municipality of Vienna: cutting the city's material footprint from 14t toward the EU target of 7t per capita through the food system.

  • App prototype: a consumer app with product scanning, smart food swaps, and a city-wide impact simulator.
  • Policy frameworks: a public procurement reform covering 100,000 daily meals in Vienna's schools, hospitals, and care facilities, plus a policy brief linking individual behaviour change with institutional reform.
  • Impact modelling: projected about 390,000 tonnes of potential annual reduction.
